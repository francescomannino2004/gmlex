Guest lecture by the CEO of ENGIE Italy on geopolitics, Italy's high energy prices, the energy transition, and PPAs as a concrete solution, followed by Q&A.

### Company Overview
- French CAC 40 company, moved from coal to gas in the 60s and into renewables in the 90s
    - Present in 30 countries; 90,000 people, 8.8 billion EBIT, 57 gigawatt installed renewable capacity
- Energy transition now means three goals: decarbonization, affordability, and security of supply
- Shifting in the last five years from gas networks to power infrastructure; resilient portfolio seen as key to agility
- Exiting Belgian nuclear assets to the Belgian state, since nuclear needs huge investment and carries high operational risk

### Geopolitics and Italian Energy Prices
- Defocusing from North America because the US government changed rules, removing investment stability
- Italy imports 95% of gas and power is priced by gas, so prices sit well above the European average
- Prices are now structurally "higher for longer", breaking existing business plan assumptions
- European elections pushing energy toward state control and regulation, which the speaker views negatively

### Strategy: Electrification, Flexibility, Grid
- Core focus for the next five years: electrification, flexibility (BESS storage), and power grids
- In Italy, over 500 megawatts of BESS already authorized; medium BESS of 3 to 5 megawatts now possible on site
- Renewables produced in the south, consumed in the industrial north, so efficient transmission is critical
    - Spain's six-hour blackout cited as a grid management lesson
- Gas remains necessary for now; aim is to shift molecules toward green gas such as biomethane

### Decarbonization Trends, Barriers and AI
- Fit for 55 targets are lagging in every stream; 2025 was the first year emissions rose instead of fell
- Energy efficiency is underrated; data center waste heat linked to district heating cited as an example
- AI is already here: managers have AI coaches and finance asked for 9% lower costs from 2029 in the plan
- Data center growth limited by grid connections and permitting

### PPA Example and Q&A
- Heineken case: about 9 MW PV in Sardinia near Ichnusa plant, with on-site and offsite PPAs
    - 45% self-consumed covers the site fully; 55% sent to a northern site at a flat 10-year price
    - Current 40% production-demand match targeted to reach 75% by adding wind and storage
- Similar PPAs exist with Amazon and Google for green power at flat prices
- Speaker expects EU decarbonization targets to be slowed and extended, as happened with automotive
