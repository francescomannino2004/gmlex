Why fundraising should not be a founder's first priority: updated Italian/European VC figures, costs of raising (dilution, lost autonomy), VC-backed vs bootstrapped, and reaching product-market fit first.

### VC Market Update
- Italy near 1.7 billion invested by end of October 2026, possibly a first 2 billion euro year
- Amount invested rising but number of rounds falling as seed-stage companies mature into Series A
    - Risk: fewer seed rounds may not feed the funnel of future companies
- Europe's largest 2026 rounds (e.g. Mistral AI, 3 billion euro) dwarfed by US 444 billion euro vs Europe 68, Asia 102
- Open question: should Europe focus on strengths like robotics, life sciences, deep tech rather than core AI and software?

### The Real Cost of Fundraising
- Raising money is not a measure of success; Mailchimp, GitHub, Basecamp, Notion grew bootstrapped for years
- Raising means dilution, and high valuations raise down-round risk at the next round
- Formal autonomy loss: voting vs equity, veto rights, drag/tag-along, protective provisions on debt, board seats from Series A
    - Tag-along protects minority investors; drag-along lets majority force a full sale
    - Speaker's fund: A shares hold all votes; C shares may own 60% with no votes
- Informal loss: scaling pressure, constant reporting, obligation to spend raised money, time-consuming investor relations
    - Prefer fewer, larger investors; pre-brief board members so meetings just formalize decisions

### VC-Backed vs Bootstrapped
- VC-backed firms grow at all costs, burn cash, chase next rounds and plan exits; bootstrapped aim for profit from day one and full control
    - Example: speaker's fund exited via secondary sale at 30% discount, about 4.5x in three years
- VCs increasingly also want sustainable business models; good companies succeed either way
- Banks look for bootstrapped qualities; some VCs now offer venture debt alongside equity
- Fundraising is slow (about 100 emails per investment), distracts from running the business, and feedback must be filtered systematically

### Before Fundraising: Team and Users
- Startups begin confused about product, customers and revenue (Dave McClure quote)
- Team: transparent talks on roles and commitment, fair cap table, vesting (1-year cliff, 25%, then to 100% over 4 years), obsession with execution, focus
- Users: build something few users love (Sam Altman); use open, unbiased interview questions and iterate quickly with an MVP
- Loving users become free ambassadors and reveal willingness to pay

### Product-Market Fit and Metrics
- Product-market fit: about 40% of users would be very disappointed if the product disappeared (Sean Ellis test, cited with Lean Startup)
- Key metrics: NPS (0-6 detractors, 7-8 neutral/passives, 9-10 promoters), organic traffic, retention/churn, paying users, LTV/CAC
    - Churned users give the most valuable feedback; indifferent churn is a bad sign
- Right metrics change by stage, and product-market fit is temporary as markets, laws and competitors shift
- Building a product differs from building a company; founders must shift from product manager to business manager
