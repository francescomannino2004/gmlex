Lecture on European venture capital: market data, how VCs select and monitor deals, entrepreneur/VC conflicts, and the debt vs equity contract setup leading into a moral hazard model.

### European VC Market Context
- European VC market at 35.3 billion in 2025, heavily weighted to ICT, vs 320 billion in the US.
- Europe consumes rather than produces AI, raising dependency concerns vis-à-vis US and China.
- Bank-based VCs and prudent-man rules on pension funds historically limited risky investment in Europe.
    - Swedish pension funds are an exception; UK long constrained by prudent man rule.
- Disappearance of new markets (Nuovo Mercato, Nouveau Marché) removed easy exit routes for small firms.

### Angels vs VCs
- Angels vary by industry and entrepreneurial experience: financial angels focus on returns, experienced angels add mentoring.
- Angels can syndicate or create special vehicles to match VC-sized rounds and compete with VCs.
- European angels typically have less industry/entrepreneurial depth, so are less active in firms.

### How VCs Select and Add Value
- Gompers et al. (2020) survey of ~1,000 VCs vs Kaplan (2025) with 8,000 deals; VC networks source most deals, 30% self-generated.
- 47% cite management team as key selection factor; jockey matters early, product/market matter in later rounds.
- Selection takes ~83 days and ~118 hours of due diligence; success attributed partly to luck (statistically significant).
- Post-investment: weekly interaction, strategic guidance dominant; investment decisions typically unanimous among GPs.

### Entrepreneur vs VC Conflicts
- Entrepreneurs want control and shared risk; VCs want financial return, later-round participation, and eventual liquidity via IPO.
- Contract clauses (voting, board, liquidation, anti-dilution, non-compete) manage conflicts; US non-competes ~16 months vs ~6 in Europe.
- Moral hazard runs both ways: entrepreneur may slack post-funding, VC may invest passively to deploy LP capital.
- Replacement of founder by CEO happens via contract clauses that shift voting power to VC when things go badly.

### Debt vs Equity and Modigliani-Miller
- Debt gives downside protection (paid first, flat payoff); equity gives upside exposure after debt is repaid.
- Equity incentivizes both entrepreneur and VC to push return above D; debt lenders only want collateral back.
- Modigliani-Miller: with no market imperfections, capital structure is irrelevant; just maximize expected return.
- Real markets have taxation, asymmetric information, moral hazard, so asset choice matters, motivating the upcoming model.
