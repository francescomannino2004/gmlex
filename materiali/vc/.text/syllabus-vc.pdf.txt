MISSION
Innovative technology companies are a key driver of growth in advanced economies. They
represent the most dynamic segment of entrepreneurial activity and make significant contributions
to job creation, higher living standards, and wealth generation. They also play a crucial role in the
continued growth of large, established firms, which often acquire start-ups to gain access to new
products and technologies.
A central challenge for entrepreneurs is securing resources they do not directly control, particularly
financial capital. Investors are critical in this process, as funding not only provides liquidity but also
attracts other essential resources such as skilled labor and specialized assets. These investors
form the core of the venture capital ecosystem, which has expanded rapidly worldwide over the
past decade, fueled by the pursuit of high returns in illiquid markets.
To successfully raise capital, entrepreneurs must develop a clear understanding of the venture
capital landscape and the different types of investors within it, such as VC funds, venture capital
trusts (VCTs), small business investment companies (SBICs), business angels, incubators, and
club deals.
The mission of this course is therefore twofold:
a) to analyze and understand how innovative entrepreneurs secure financing, including financial
contracts, company valuation, and the implications for innovation strategy; and
b) to examine the defining characteristics of venture investors, covering legal considerations,
taxation issues, and the key aspects of deal-making.

The final grade is based on a 50%-50% structure for students attending the two partial exams:
• 50%: First mid-term exam covering Parts One and Two (MCQs)
• 50%: Second mid-term exam covering Part Three (MCQs)
Students who opt for the general exam will be required to answer questions covering all three parts
of the course.
The exam questions will require students to either discuss specific topics covered during the
course or apply the concepts to numerical exercises and mini-cases. The overall objective of the
assessment is to evaluate students' ability to collect and organize information and data, and to
apply them in solving real-world problems typically faced by venture capital funds, investors, and
VC-backed entrepreneurs.
