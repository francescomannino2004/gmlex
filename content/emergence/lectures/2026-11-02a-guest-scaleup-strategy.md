Guest lecture by Vittorio on scaling up growth-stage companies, using a hypothetical 100 million revenue internal-communications SaaS case to debate investment timing, Microsoft and AI risk, organization, and sales.

### Framing: Growth Investing and the Case
- Speaker drew on consulting, a scale-up that became Vodafone, government, and now growth private equity (not venture).
    - Agenda: scale-up strategy, organization and team, expansion (geography, product, adjacencies), financing
- Growth stage: significant revenue (50 to 300-400 million), little debt, a defensible moat, judged by the rule of 40.
    - Rule of 40 = revenue growth % + EBITDA margin %; rule of 20-25 needs a fix
- Case: internal-comms SaaS, 10 years old, 2 founders, 700 people, 100 million revenue, break-even, 10-15% growth (rule of 15).
    - 50% Europe, 40% USA; integrated web, email and app; less than a euro per seat per month

### Timing, Adoption and Moat
- Key questions: when it will deliver, cost, financing, adoption speed, and whether there is a real moat.
    - Free-to-premium works for some products, others never monetize if free
- Growth deals have models and data, but tech evolution and adoption remain uncertain; expert calls often polarized.
- Examples: level 4 driverless cars, robotaxis likely a mixed human/robot fleet given peak-hour capital cost.
- Mis-estimated adoption: Spotify underestimated, Duolingo up then down, Revolut, Peloton lost 95%, Match, Zoom lacked a moat.
    - Competitive disruption: Novo Nordisk vs Eli Lilly, Tesla vs BYD, China in autonomy

### Case Debate: Category Leader vs Microsoft Risk
- Bull view: integrated product defines the category; focused founders out-execute a distracted Microsoft.
- Bear view: Microsoft could easily add the feature or give it free, as happened to Zoom.
- Only about 10-15% of students said they would invest at this stage.

### Organization and Culture in Scale-ups
- Startup phase needs adventurers and risk takers; growth phase needs curious planners with business cases.
    - Expect and accept healthy conflict between the two cultures; keep the DNA
- Wireless Logic left 15 acquired CEOs independent, causing product complexity; now centralizing the product plan.
- At Vodafone: 70-30 split of common plan vs local innovation; M-Pesa in Kenya.
    - A separate low-cost brand unit in Milan failed when corporate functions blocked it
- Sustained scale-up culture is rare; some large firms (Microsoft, Google, JP Morgan) do it reasonably.

### Case Debate: AI, Sales and the Buyer
- AI split views: better targeted content vs easy replication by customers or cheap competitors.
    - Survivors: systems of record, and products where building in-house is uneconomic for mid-size firms
- At under a euro per seat, a 1,000-person firm pays about 8,000 to 12,000 euros per year, so building in-house rarely pays.
- "Data must have legs": analytics matter only with a strong salesforce acting on them.
- Decision maker unclear: HR favors engagement, IT resists complexity, internal comms may defer to Microsoft.
