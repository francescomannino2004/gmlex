Lecture on venture capital financing: how startups match with banks, angels, VCs, and government funding, why VC selection vs value-add is hard to disentangle, and how European regulation, bankruptcy law, and exit conditions constrain the industry.

### Framing: Supply vs Demand for VC
- Draghi report highlights that VC scarcity is not only a supply-of-funds problem but also a demand and quality-of-demand problem
- Comparing tiny startups to mature firms distorts growth measurement; EIF-backed survey found limited measurable effect

### Financing Sources by Novelty and Capital
- Low novelty, low capital: personal credit and bank loans dominate
- High novelty: angels and VCs step in because banks reject uncertainty
- High capital, low novelty: commercial banks; high capital, high novelty: VCs and government (e.g. AI infrastructure)
- Financing is a matching problem driven by risk, information, control, capital intensity, and follow-on needs

### Bootstrapping, Banks and the 4 Cs
- Bootstrapping via savings or friends and family is harder in Europe than in the US, so entrepreneurs tend to be older
- Banks assess the 4 Cs: capacity, collateral, conditions of the economy, and capital invested
- Banks require ~3 years of financial statements plus personal statements as collateral, incompatible with startup reality
- Debt contracts limit downside but exclude the upside; startups have negative cash flow, intangible assets, and information asymmetry

### VC Structure and Types
- VC firms raise from limited partners; general partners typically decide investments, with education and technical knowledge being critical
- Types: financial/independent VCs (incl. micro/seed), corporate/strategic VCs, public/government VCs, and family offices
- Strategic VCs may invest to expand market access or to neutralize a competitor by absorbing its R&D
- Early European VCs were mostly bank-backed, reasoned like banks, and were not active investors

### VC Value-Add, Selection and Syndication
- VCs contribute expertise, networks, follow-on capital, managerial access, and certification for later bank financing
    - Risk: even without stealing, VCs can spread ideas across their portfolio, so patenting matters
- Survey data: VC-backed firms outperform, but this may reflect selection rather than value-add; econometrics needed to separate
- Strategic advice and networking are the largest reported VC contributions in both startup and expansion stages
- Syndication motives: better selection early, resolving information asymmetry later, risk sharing, complementary expertise, and navigating cross-border European deals
