Lecture on working inside versus outside the comfort zone, using war photographer Robert Capa as a parallel for founders: get close, take reasonable risks, keep expanding your comfort zone.

### Comfort Zone Basics
- Comfort zone means low effort, low stress and low risk, but rarely exciting results or career growth
- Staying comfortable is fine in some life phases, leaving more time for family, health and hobbies
- Leaving it brings new skills, novelty and earning potential, but also greater risk of loss

### Robert Capa as Case Study
- Born Endre Friedmann in Budapest, 1913; fled to Paris and reinvented himself as a war photographer
- D-Day Omaha Beach photo: he landed ahead of soldiers, back to enemy fire, shooting a manual Leica
- Blurry shots became the book 'Slightly Out of Focus'; the photo is the tip of the iceberg of preparation
- Died stepping on a landmine minutes after his last photo in Indochina; risk grows with what you put in

### Lessons for Founders
- 'If your pictures aren't good enough, you're not close enough': stay close to your market
- Comfort zone should grow daily; aim to become comfortable with being uncomfortable
- Example: founders handling a lawsuit from driving schools without crumbling
- Half measures rarely build great companies; co-founders should not have side gigs

### Q&A and Personal Example
- Pushing past discomfort (e.g. job applications that feel out of reach) always pays off
- Speaker got a fund principal role by approaching the managing partner right after an LP meeting
- Most people projecting genius are good at self-presentation; research roles to judge real fit
- Balance humility and confidence; avoid crossing into cockiness
