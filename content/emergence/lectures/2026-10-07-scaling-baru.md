Lecture on why scaling ventures is hard: founder imprinting, managerial attention limits (Penrose), and the need to formalize during growth, applied to the Baru pet-services case.

### Defining Scaling
- Scaling means exceptional growth, ideally output growing faster than inputs; restaurant chains scale poorly.
- Scaling is a strategic choice; many founders avoid it to keep control.
    - Only about 3.5% of ~700,000 new US firms grow to large size
    - OECD: fast growth is 20% yearly over 3 years
- Class poll: about 66% chose a consolidation year, about 17% expansion; challenges named included hiring and market demand.

### Imprinting and Founder Blueprints
- Early founding choices become rules, routines and hierarchies that are rarely revisited.
- Stanford study of 200 tech startups mapped blueprints on attachment, selection, and coordination/control.
    - Founder blueprint predicts the organization better than current CEO's, though evidence is weak
    - At 50 employees: 17.1% managers (commitment) vs about 37% (bureaucracy)
- Study of 167 Silicon Valley startups: founding structure and founder experience strongly predict later executive structure.

### Imprinting in the Baru Case
- Hourly employee contracts worked early but caused problems once demand grew.
- Luxury building partnerships benefited buildings, not Baru; concierges lacked incentives and sometimes promoted competitors.
- Off-the-shelf booking app fell into disuse; contractual social events and luxury service became burdens in new cities.

### Allocation: Managerial Attention (Penrose)
- Growth is limited by managers' finite attention, since new hires need training in tacit culture and processes.
- Baru's scarcest resource by summer 2016 was management attention, worsened by founders' maternity leaves and city differences.
- Evidence: fast growth predicts slower later growth only with high tacitness or geographic expansion.
- Hiring five managers at once would not help, as training them consumes remaining leadership time.

### Adaptation: Formalization During Growth
- US internet ventures 1996 to 2001: formalization and administrative intensity correlated with success (correlational).
- India field experiment with 100 founders: pairing with a formal-management peer improved performance; survival up 10%.
- Baru formalized via outsourced 24/7 hotline and scheduling, losing personalization; workers became overworked.
- Students suggested HR leadership and multi-city hires; culture shifted from pet passion to a job.
