Class discussion of the Amazon case: groups analyzed the pre-internet book retail industry, Barnes & Noble's capabilities, Amazon's entry strategy and B&N's failed online response, concluding with sticky capabilities and dynamic capabilities.

### Book Retail Industry: Good or Bad?
- Market worth 26 billion, expected to reach 33 billion; demand peaks in Q4 and is hard to predict
    - 50,000 new titles yearly, about a million titles in stock
- Publishers: about 40,000, but top 20 hold 88% share; author royalties around 10 to 15%
- Wholesalers (Ingram over 50%, Baker and Taylor) existed to serve small mom-and-pop stores; net margin about 1.5%
    - Returns around 30% of hardcovers, doubling shipping costs
- Verdict: decent, growing industry for dominant players, but low margins, high entry barriers and competition from TV, films, video games

### Barnes & Noble's Capabilities
- Largest US book retailer, about 2.5 billion sales, roughly 2 to 2.5% net margin, superstore format
- Key capabilities: centralized procurement and warehousing, experiential marketing (cafes, Starbucks, events), IT for logistics
    - Average purchase $20 vs $10 at independent stores
- Real estate finance mattered: long-term leases kept invested capital near 600 million instead of $3 billion

### Amazon's Entry Strategy
- Bezos targeted the internet (web growth about 2,000% per year); books were a way in, chosen from about 20 categories
- Books fit: no 800-pound gorilla (Borders plus B&N about 14%), huge title count, standardized products
    - 'Sell all, carry few': list 1 million titles, own about 2,000; return rate about 2%
- Software-centric firm: 80% of software spend on back office, managers with computer backgrounds, based in Seattle for tech talent
- Key trick: securing supply from Ingram, a wholesaler in decline seeking new channels

### Barnes & Noble's Reaction and Takeaways
- barnesandnoble.com was kept separate for tax reasons and underpushed due to cannibalization fears and store manager incentives
- Missed opportunities: in-store pickup, attacking Ingram as Amazon's weak point
- **Lesson: capabilities are sticky; ambidexterity alone is not enough, firms need dynamic capabilities and agility** (Teece et al.)
