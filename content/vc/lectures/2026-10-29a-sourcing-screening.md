Lecture on VC deal sourcing, screening criteria, Italian VC data, and early-stage valuation methods, ending with a scoring exercise on the Endorsi case.

### Deal Sourcing Channels
- Sourcing is mostly network driven: fellow VCs, known founders, lawyers, accountants, dinners, events, and 'enablers'.
    - Co-investing builds reciprocity; VCs often back founders again after a well-executed failure (8 out of 10 fail)
- 2020 paper (data from about 2015-16): about 200 deals seen per 5 considered in California; only 2% sourced quantitatively.
- Accelerators like Y Combinator act as a certification, but cold outreach still needs referrals to vet founders' business reputation.
- Speaker passed on an AI sentiment startup after a trusted contact reported the founder scammed a business partner.

### Data Tools and Regional Differences
- A data-driven fund modeled outliers, not the mean, using LinkedIn hiring and web traffic data.
    - Limits: gut feeling is hard to quantify; top engineers may not be on LinkedIn
    - Other tools: SignalFire's Beacon (10 million data sources), Affinity (relationship intelligence)
- Algorithms shortlist companies, but humans decide; investors are chosen for value add, not just money.
- Europe is country-based with rising cross-border deals; Latin America is very cross-border and eyes US exits.
- Continental Europe lacks late-stage capital and IPO routes, so exits rely on acquisitions and CVCs.
    - Market cap on GDP: UK around 200%, Italy historically 35-38, 47 this year

### Screening and Italian VC Data
- About half of screening weight is the team; also fit with fund strategy, product, business model thinking, market and TAM.
    - Checklist: stage, ticket size, industry, geography, opportunity size, key assumptions, competition, management adaptability
- Italian startup incorporation 'for 1 euro' really takes about a month and costs in stamps, notary and banking hurdles.
- Italy study: 84 companies out of 120; more public and early-stage VCs, more healthcare focus.
    - TAM cited by only 1% and business model 2%, since many Italian VCs exit via secondary sales to private equity

### Early-Stage Valuation Methods
- Four methods covered: Berkus, scorecard, risk factor summation (12 categories), and VC method; results rarely converge.
- Berkus five categories: sound idea, prototype, management team, strategic relationships, product rollout to market.
    - Classic version 100K each (500K max); rescaled to 500K each (2.5 million max), as average seed is about 3.5 million
- Scorecard uses comparables by sub-industry, geography and stage, adjusted by seven factors; finding true comps is hard.
- Top scores: real willingness to pay, a filed patent, founders with previous exits, pilots and credible go-to-market.

### Endorsi Case Exercise
- Harvard case (10-12 years old): Endorsi, Australia-based digital endorsements startup, founder relocating to SF.
- Class scoring: idea 400, prototype well below average, team 300 (two people plus part-time, willing to relocate).
- Strategic relationships weak: some personal network, but the founders appear desperate for investors.
