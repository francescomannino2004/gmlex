Later-stage startup valuation: trading multiples, precedent transactions with control premium, and calibrating to the prior round, applied to a fictional Series C case.

### Silicon Valley Origin Story
- Silicon Valley grew from a 'fluke': Shockley (transistor Nobel winner) set up there to be near his sick mother.
    - Employees (the 'Traitorous Eight') left over his temper and seeded firms like Intel, AMD
- Alumni networks (e.g. the PayPal mafia) went on to found or fund Tesla, SpaceX, Uber, Meta.

### Early vs Later Stage Valuation
- Later stage brings real numbers and listed peers; assumptions and comparables matter more than the formulas.
- Methods covered: trading multiples, precedent transactions, DCF, and scenario analysis (First Chicago method).
- Drawbacks: complex capital structures (liquidation preferences, share classes) and illiquidity discounts.
    - Square case preview: founders got about 0.5% at a roughly 3 billion exit
- Investors care more about IRR at later stages, when forecasts become meaningful.

### Series C Case and Trading Multiples
- Fictional vertical software company (Organise): Series B June 2025, 30 million raised at 210 post-money.
    - Series C: 60 million at 450 pre-money for an 11.8% stake
    - NTM revenue 54 million, growth 50%, gross margin 76%, net cash 22 million
- EV = equity value + debt − cash; equity value = EV + cash − debt. With no debt, only cash matters.
- Rule of 40 (revenue growth + FCF margin) justifies negative FCF (−10) during heavy growth investment.
- Multiples from 8 peers: median 7.6x, regression on growth 10.4x (R² 74%), growth-adjusted 14.4x.
    - Pre-money equity = multiple × 54 × (1 − 0.20 DLOM) + 22 cash; DLOM range 15 to 25%

### Precedent Transactions and Control
- Control premium study (US listed targets, 1980 to 2008): median 38%, average 46%; 35% assumed, range 25 to 45%.
- Precedent multiples are stripped of control premium; a buyer seeking control would add it back (×1.35).
- Clean valuation differs from willingness to pay, which adds for rights like liquidation preference, board seat, or top co-investors.
- Unicorn study of 135 firms: 56% average overvaluation of common shares; 65 worth below 1 billion after adjustment.

### Calibrating to the Prior Round
- Steps: derive EV implied by the Series B, divide by revenue then, compare to peer multiple, get calibration factor.
- Apply factor (about 0.85) to the 10.4x regression multiple; no separate DLOM since it is embedded in the factor.
- Drawback: any bias in the previous round carries into the new valuation.
- Slide revenue figure was wrong; it should be the NTM revenue forecast as of June 2025.
