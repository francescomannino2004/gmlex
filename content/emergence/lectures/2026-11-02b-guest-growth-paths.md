Guest lecture (part 2) on scale-up growth paths (geography, product, adjacencies), funding choices and exits, using a live SaaS case later revealed as the speaker's own company, plus leadership traits.

### Three Growth Paths and Their Traps
- Growth options: new geographies, product extensions, or adjacent businesses (e.g. restaurant to hotel)
- Geography checks: business model, local competitors, hiring/culture, channels, regulation; Europe is often a trap
- Product extension needs research budget and engineering cost; selling to a new buyer can double commercial cost
- Adjacencies can destroy value, but work when channels or technology platform are shared

### Portfolio Examples
- French health-booking firm (likely Doctolib) struggled in Italy, succeeded in Germany
    - Italian doctors paid fixed per patient, not per appointment; small practices; private hospitals refused to share slots
- Phone refurbisher (Back Market) moving into e-bikes added repair and logistics complexity, limited success
- Wireless Logic moved from IoT to travel eSIMs: same core competence and channel, very successful

### Class Debate on the SaaS Case
- Ideas: improve product and sales funnel; expand to Southeast Asia, Latin America, Africa at low cost
- Software travels at near zero cost, but sales channels, agents and commissions are not free
- Adding HR or task management risks competing with SAP and Microsoft and doing many things poorly
- Internal comms consulting seen as promising since process is not proprietary; custom builds don't scale (compared to Celonis)

### Funding Rounds and Exits
- Highest valuation is fine only for a last round; a later down round hurts stock-option hiring
- Few large vs many small investors: control and network vs governance complexity; no single right answer
- Cost of capital: equity 15-20%, debt 8-9%, hybrid 12-13%; equity buys expertise and dry powder
- Exits: IPO, trade sale, roll-up, leverage, dividends; founder secondary sales signal confidence levels

### Case Reveal and Leadership Traits
- Case company: Staffbase (CEO Martin Böhringer); the speaker is chairman
    - Cut staff via AI, consolidator in sector, IPO paused, active in Europe, US, Asia-Pacific
- Student exit votes: M&A consolidation, sell to Microsoft, fix business structure and sales
- Successful leaders: product perfectionists, non-dogmatic on strategy, people-focused, medium-term vision
- Keep 'listening antennas': read widely and spend lots of time with team and partners
