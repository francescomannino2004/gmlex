Fundraising preparation (investor updates, pitch deck, one-pager, data room, pitching), followed by a breakout where students presented early-stage histories of Notion, Patreon and others.

### Preparing to Fundraise
- Send warm contacts a short, regular update (monthly to quarterly) with real milestones: features, clients, key hires.
    - Signals momentum and organization, helps you land one of the ~10 investor meetings
- Target funds by sector and stage; sending a software deck to a life science fund wastes everyone's time.
- Pitch deck must be very concise and clear; investors see thousands each year, so deep homework is needed behind it.
- Prepare a one-pager: team, market, need, product and solution, traction, cap table.

### Data Room, Momentum and Pitching
- A data room is a well-structured folder: contracts, patents, financials, pipeline, office leases. Have it ready before fundraising.
- Delaying key documents kills investor momentum; aim to share the data room the same or next day when asked.
- Practice the pitch in a mirror, on video, and with honest friends; be confident, not cocky.

### Course Assessment (from side discussion)
- Group assignment is a PDF memo with up to 5 exhibits, no slides required.
- **Group work 40%, exam 60%; participation gives up to 1.5 bonus points.**

### Breakout: Company Case Studies
- Notion: friends as team and users, burned 2 million, rebuilt in Japan, then raised 10 million (2019), 50 million (2020), 275 (2021).
    - Lesson: friends give biased feedback and are not the target users
- Patreon (Jack Conte, Sam Yam): grew via YouTube, seed of about 2.5 million after five months; first 40 creators emailed rejected them.
- Psychology service for expats via Facebook groups: bootstrapped 2019, 150,000 euro in 2020, 17 million in 2023.
- A coffee chain tested one shop as a lab for seven months, profitable from early shops, later raised from General Atlantic.

### Closing Takeaways
- Fundraising should not be the first priority; solve a problem, build team, know users and product first.
- Raising money means giving away equity, autonomy and time.
- Product market fit is measurable: NPS, retention, churn; track the one number that matters now.
- Use warm intros and build investor relationships even when not raising.
