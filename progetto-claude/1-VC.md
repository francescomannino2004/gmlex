### VC 29-09 (lex 07) — Fonti di finanziamento e ruolo dei VC — GIÀ SUPERATO
_Data: 2026-09-29 · Argomento del syllabus 7: Financing Innovation: Angel Investors and VCs_

Lecture on venture capital financing: how startups match with banks, angels, VCs, and government funding, why VC selection vs value-add is hard to disentangle, and how European regulation, bankruptcy law, and exit conditions constrain the industry.

#### Framing: Supply vs Demand for VC
- Draghi report highlights that VC scarcity is not only a supply-of-funds problem but also a demand and quality-of-demand problem
- Comparing tiny startups to mature firms distorts growth measurement; EIF-backed survey found limited measurable effect

#### Financing Sources by Novelty and Capital
- Low novelty, low capital: personal credit and bank loans dominate
- High novelty: angels and VCs step in because banks reject uncertainty
- High capital, low novelty: commercial banks; high capital, high novelty: VCs and government (e.g. AI infrastructure)
- Financing is a matching problem driven by risk, information, control, capital intensity, and follow-on needs

#### Bootstrapping, Banks and the 4 Cs
- Bootstrapping via savings or friends and family is harder in Europe than in the US, so entrepreneurs tend to be older
- Banks assess the 4 Cs: capacity, collateral, conditions of the economy, and capital invested
- Banks require ~3 years of financial statements plus personal statements as collateral, incompatible with startup reality
- Debt contracts limit downside but exclude the upside; startups have negative cash flow, intangible assets, and information asymmetry

#### VC Structure and Types
- VC firms raise from limited partners; general partners typically decide investments, with education and technical knowledge being critical
- Types: financial/independent VCs (incl. micro/seed), corporate/strategic VCs, public/government VCs, and family offices
- Strategic VCs may invest to expand market access or to neutralize a competitor by absorbing its R&D
- Early European VCs were mostly bank-backed, reasoned like banks, and were not active investors

#### VC Value-Add, Selection and Syndication
- VCs contribute expertise, networks, follow-on capital, managerial access, and certification for later bank financing
    - Risk: even without stealing, VCs can spread ideas across their portfolio, so patenting matters
- Survey data: VC-backed firms outperform, but this may reflect selection rather than value-add; econometrics needed to separate
- Strategic advice and networking are the largest reported VC contributions in both startup and expansion stages
- Syndication motives: better selection early, resolving information asymmetry later, risk sharing, complementary expertise, and navigating cross-border European deals

### VC 30-09 (lex 08) — VC europeo, selezione, debito vs equity — GIÀ SUPERATO
_Data: 2026-09-30 · Argomento del syllabus 8: How do VCs choose and how do they affect firms' decisions_

Lecture on European venture capital: market data, how VCs select and monitor deals, entrepreneur/VC conflicts, and the debt vs equity contract setup leading into a moral hazard model.

#### European VC Market Context
- European VC market at 35.3 billion in 2025, heavily weighted to ICT, vs 320 billion in the US.
- Europe consumes rather than produces AI, raising dependency concerns vis-à-vis US and China.
- Bank-based VCs and prudent-man rules on pension funds historically limited risky investment in Europe.
    - Swedish pension funds are an exception; UK long constrained by prudent man rule.
- Disappearance of new markets (Nuovo Mercato, Nouveau Marché) removed easy exit routes for small firms.

#### Angels vs VCs
- Angels vary by industry and entrepreneurial experience: financial angels focus on returns, experienced angels add mentoring.
- Angels can syndicate or create special vehicles to match VC-sized rounds and compete with VCs.
- European angels typically have less industry/entrepreneurial depth, so are less active in firms.

#### How VCs Select and Add Value
- Gompers et al. (2020) survey of ~1,000 VCs vs Kaplan (2025) with 8,000 deals; VC networks source most deals, 30% self-generated.
- 47% cite management team as key selection factor; jockey matters early, product/market matter in later rounds.
- Selection takes ~83 days and ~118 hours of due diligence; success attributed partly to luck (statistically significant).
- Post-investment: weekly interaction, strategic guidance dominant; investment decisions typically unanimous among GPs.

#### Entrepreneur vs VC Conflicts
- Entrepreneurs want control and shared risk; VCs want financial return, later-round participation, and eventual liquidity via IPO.
- Contract clauses (voting, board, liquidation, anti-dilution, non-compete) manage conflicts; US non-competes ~16 months vs ~6 in Europe.
- Moral hazard runs both ways: entrepreneur may slack post-funding, VC may invest passively to deploy LP capital.
- Replacement of founder by CEO happens via contract clauses that shift voting power to VC when things go badly.

#### Debt vs Equity and Modigliani-Miller
- Debt gives downside protection (paid first, flat payoff); equity gives upside exposure after debt is repaid.
- Equity incentivizes both entrepreneur and VC to push return above D; debt lenders only want collateral back.
- Modigliani-Miller: with no market imperfections, capital structure is irrelevant; just maximize expected return.
- Real markets have taxation, asymmetric information, moral hazard, so asset choice matters, motivating the upcoming model.

#### Decisions Made
- Course model will cover single-source moral hazard only, not double moral hazard, to keep the logic tractable.

### VC 6-10 — Modello di moral hazard (Holmström-Tirole) — GIÀ SUPERATO
_Data: 2026-10-06 · Argomento del syllabus 9: The Deal Structure: Contracts and Incentives (I)_

Lecture on VC contracting via a single moral hazard model (likely Holmström and Tirole): incentive and investor constraints, why Europe needs more founder assets than the US, and why VC contracts are equity-based.

#### Course Admin and Study Approach
- Class 8 contract lecture notes are released only after class so students work actively and build the model together
- ChatGPT and lecture notes are fine if used actively; passive study leads to far more mistakes
- Class 7 graph with inverted entrepreneurial activity axis has been corrected; students should recheck it

#### Why VC Contracts Matter
- Contracts align entrepreneur and VC objectives when moral hazard arises; debt and equity alone cannot do this
- Contracts must also set monitoring and control, VC effort incentives, return split, and the liquidity event exit
    - Exit is regulated since a founder or VC leaving right after an IPO sends a bad market signal
- VC deals are conditional contracting: they must cover all possible outcomes, unlike a standard bank loan

#### Single Moral Hazard Model
- Project costs I; entrepreneur has assets A < I, borrows I − A, protected by limited liability (no collateral)
- Return R with probability P, else 0; shirking lowers P from PH to PL but gives private benefit B
    - B and PL are independent and exogenous; higher B only raises the reward needed
- NPV must be positive only when behaving: PH·R − I > 0, while PL·R − I + B < 0
- IC constraint: (PH − PL)·R_B ≥ B; IR constraint: PH·R_L ≥ I − A, equality under perfect investor competition
    - Investor only breaks even, so the entrepreneur captures the full NPV as the price of incentives

#### Required Founder Assets and US vs Europe
- Solving for A: A ≥ I − PH·(R − B/(PH − PL)); higher I needs more A, higher R needs less
- If PH equals PL, A goes to infinity: investors cannot distinguish behavior, so no one lends
- Higher B raises required assets; slow courts and opaque, unreliable economies raise B (e.g. Argentina 2001)
- Explains why US founders get funding with little capital while in Europe it relies on private savings
    - Example: US students borrowing from banks for MIT tuition, education debt not dischargeable in bankruptcy

#### Debt vs Equity
- With zero payoff on failure, debt and equity are indistinguishable since there is no downside protection
- If value remains on failure, give the leftover (debt) to the investor and equity to the entrepreneur for upside incentive
- Under double moral hazard both need upside incentives, so VC contracts are typically all-equity

### VC 7-10 — Term sheet, pre/post-money, preferred stock — GIÀ SUPERATO
_Data: 2026-10-07 · Argomento del syllabus 10: The Deal Structure (II)_

VC lecture recapping the moral hazard financing model, then covering term sheets, pre/post-money valuation, and preferred stock features (liquidation preference, redemption, conversion, participation); a new case session on a CEO dispute began.

#### Moral Hazard Model Recap
- Investor rationality constraint: expected return to lender, PH(R − R_B), must be at least I − A.
- Required entrepreneur assets rise with project cost and private benefit B, fall with return; PH equal to PL means no one lends.
    - Weaker, slower courts (Europe vs US) raise B, so Europe demands more collateral
- With zero payoff on failure, debt vs equity is irrelevant; with leftover value, debt protects the investor.
- Double moral hazard (entrepreneur and VC both need incentives) points to equity rather than plain debt.

#### Term Sheets and Valuation
- Term sheet is a preliminary agreement; bargaining power shifts with market cycles (VCs held power in the 2008 credit crunch).
- Post-money = pre-money + investment; example: 8 million pre, 2 million invested, 10 million post, investor share 20%.
    - Analysts quote post-money; investors think in pre-money then add the investment
- Differing national laws hinder cross-border VC; EU's proposed '28th regime' and US Delaware incorporation mentioned.
- Contracts also cover anti-dilution, covenants and conditional terms, and employee terms like 16-month non-competes.

#### Preferred Stock Features
- VCs use preferred stock, paid before common, giving some downside protection without being debt.
- Liquidation preference multiplier: 95.8% of US deals in Q2 2026 used 1x; higher multiples squeeze founders.
- Unconditional redemption rights signal strong investor power and can force liquidation; only 5.4% of deals used them.
- Participating preferred lets investors also share in the founders' residual; 96.4% of deals were non-participating.

#### Convertible Preferred Example
- 2 million at 5 dollars per share gives 400k shares; with 600k existing, investor owns 40%.
- Convert when 40% of exit value exceeds 2 million, so above 5 million; a 2x multiple shifts the threshold right.
- Asking for convertibility implies the investor expects post-money of at least 5 million, so pre-money 3 million.
- Caps can limit conversion upside; these terms push VCs to add value toward higher valuations.

### VC 13-10 — Clausole contrattuali, SAFE, anti-dilution, staging — GIÀ SUPERATO
_Data: 2026-10-13 · Argomento del syllabus 10: The Deal Structure (II)_

Lecture on VC contract terms: preferred stock variants, SAFEs and convertible notes, anti-dilution clauses, other term sheet rights, and a stage financing example showing entrepreneurs can sell less equity.

#### Preferred Stock Variants
- Straight preferred is paid first at liquidation (after debt); preferred term = investment × multiplier, plus possible cumulative dividends.
    - Multiplier was 1 in more than 90% of US cases in Q2 2026; higher in downturns
- Redeemable preferred (about 5% of deals) lets the VC demand money back, signaling very high VC bargaining power.
- Convertible preferred: the conversion share S reveals the minimum post-money valuation as I / S; a higher multiplier raises the conversion threshold.
    - Conversion is irreversible: once converted, there is no going back to preferred
- Participating preferred keeps PT plus a share of remaining returns, often capped; beyond the cap the investor must convert to common.

#### Convertible Notes and SAFEs
- Convertible notes and SAFEs (simple agreement for future equity) are debt-like, used at pre-seed, giving little control.
    - Over 90% of pre-seed rounds in Q1 2026 used these; not used 10 years ago
- SAFE holders usually cannot demand anti-dilution: a 10% stake falls to 8% if a new investor takes 20%.

#### Anti-Dilution Clauses
- Full ratchet resets conversion price to the new issue price, punishing the entrepreneur and signaling strong VC power.
    - Example: price drops from 1 dollar to 0.5, investor shares double to 2 million, stake above 50%
- Weighted average multiplies the old price by a ratio below 1; in the example new price is 0.988 and stake falls from 50% to about 49%.
- Common exam mistake: forgetting to multiply the ratio by the old price when it equals 1.

#### Other Term Sheet Rights
- Covered pay to play, protection provisions, preemption, right of first refusal, tag along, drag along, co-sale, and lock-up periods.
- Drag along forces all shareholders to sell once the board majority decides, e.g. to liquidate a failing firm and limit liability.
- Also: information and visitation rights, key man insurance, jurisdiction choice, IP transfer, veto and affirmative rights, registration rights.
- Control rights: about 47% of US board seats held by VCs, rising above 50% when things go badly.

#### Stage Financing and Grandstanding
- Example needs 30 (10 now, 20 later); good news (prob 2/3) yields 150, bad news yields 150 with prob 1/10, else 0.
- All money up front: stake is 30/105, about 28.5%; staged: 10% at time 0 plus 13.3% at time 1, total 23.3%.
    - Staged stakes use conditional expectation on good news, since VC won't fund after bad news
- Milestones (patent, sales, profit) gate later tranches; staging serves control, signaling, and screening.
- Young VCs push IPOs early to build reputation: grandstanding.

### VC 29-10 (parte 1) — Deal sourcing, screening, Berkus

Lecture on VC deal sourcing, screening criteria, Italian VC data, and early-stage valuation methods, ending with a scoring exercise on the Endorsi case.

#### Deal Sourcing Channels
- Sourcing is mostly network driven: fellow VCs, known founders, lawyers, accountants, dinners, events, and 'enablers'.

    - Limits: gut feeling is hard to quantify; top engineers may not be on LinkedIn
    - Other tools: SignalFire's Beacon (10 million data sources), Affinity (relationship intelligence)
- Algorithms shortlist companies, but humans decide; investors are chosen for value add, not just money.
- Europe is country-based with rising cross-border deals; Latin America is very cross-border and eyes US exits.
- Continental Europe lacks late-stage capital and IPO routes, so exits rely on acquisitions and CVCs.
    - Market cap on GDP: UK around 200%, Italy historically 35-38, 47 this year

#### Screening and Italian VC Data
- About half of screening weight is the team; also fit with fund strategy, product, business model thinking, market and TAM.

    - Classic version 100K each (500K max); rescaled to 500K each (2.5 million max), as average seed is about 3.5 million
- Scorecard uses comparables by sub-industry, geography and stage, adjusted by seven factors; finding true comps is hard.
- Top scores: real willingness to pay, a filed patent, founders with previous exits, pilots and credible go-to-market.

### VC 29-10 (parte 2) — Casi Berkus e metodo Scorecard
_Data: 2026-10-29 · Argomento del syllabus 18: Investing: Valuation Methods I (early stage: Berkus, Scorecard, RFS, VC method)_

Valuation class: startup cases using a 0 to 500 per-category method (Berkus), then the scorecard method with worked examples.

#### Per-category valuation method and Arditia Lab case
- Case slides are old: judge each case in its own era; solutions that came later don't count.
- Arditia Lab, Boston AI coding agent: idea strong (30 buyer interviews, 70% would pay 6,000 a month).
    - Prototype 100 to 200 (mock-up only); management near 0 (part-time founder, no co-founders)
    - Strategic relationships 350 to 400 (listed partner, framework contract); rollout around 250
- After scoring all categories, rebalance: be more generous where others compensate, less where a score was weak.

#### Cobalto System (warehouse robots)
- Idea about 450: urgent validated problem, but willingness to pay unknown and competition is heavy.
- Prototype about 450: working product tested, no patent in a competitive, easily copied space.
- Management 400 to 450: experienced team; open sales role not worrying at angel stage.
- Strategic relationships 400 (350 to 450 range): partnership signed, no investor ties; rollout 350 to 400 with paid pilots.

#### Baby monitoring startup
- Patented noise detection lets parents monitor babies and compare sleep patterns; free app plus paid subscription and device.
- Check that the upfront device sale alone covers product, acquisition and fixed costs; subscription is then upside.
- Scores: idea 500, product 500, team 300 (benefit of doubt), strategic 100, rollout 200 (no sales plan).
- **Exam questions may have more than one correct answer, and this won't be stated.**

#### Scorecard method
- Step one: average pre-money valuation of comparable local deals (screen by geography, stage) as baseline.
- Step two: weighted adjustment factor, each category scored 0 to 2 (1 = average).
    - Team 30%, opportunity size 25%, product/technology 15%, competition 10%, sales 10%, funding needs 5%, other 5%
    - Fierce competition lowers the score
- Multiply each score by its weight, sum, then multiply baseline (example: 10 million × 1.5).
- Follow-on rounds at fast rising valuations (e.g. 330 million to 900 million in months) are a current hype trend.

#### Scorecard exercises
- San Mateo cloud developer tool, serial founders: baseline 6 million, factor 1.68, valuation 10.08 million.
- Cross-platform app platform: baseline 12 million, factor around 1.76.
    - Team 2, market about 2, product 1.6, competition 1.2, sales 1.7, funding 1.5, other 1.2
- Factor summation method is the same idea with more factors (management, stage, reputation, etc.).

    - Product: six patents but no launch infrastructure, rockets or DOD approval yet
    - Funding: no prior raise, no MVP, no letters of intent
- Competitor slide (Virgin Orbit, Boeing, SpaceX, Blue Origin) called terrible: giants are not real competitors
- Always ask why a big player would not copy you; now a key risk for AI apps versus OpenAI or Anthropic
- Adjustment factor 1.5 gives about 29.6 million, in line with the 30 million cap
    - Need at least a 15-20 percent gap to call a deal over or underpriced

#### Risk Factor Summation
- Scores range from −2 to +2; classic calibration is 250k per point on a 2 million baseline
- Factors include management, stage of business, technology, competition, sales, funding, manufacturing, legislation or politics, litigation
- Unneeded factors are simply set to zero

#### VC Method
- Post-money = exit value / (1 + R)^N, or exit value / target multiple
- Exit value is an educated guess; early stage target multiple around 50x, some VCs want three digits
- Investment capacity = exit value / target multiple − pre-money; compare against valuation

### VC 04-11 — Valutazione later-stage (multipli, precedent, calibrazione)
_Data: 2026-11-04 · Argomento del syllabus 19: Investing: Valuation Methods II (later stage: multiples, precedents, calibration)_

Later-stage startup valuation: trading multiples, precedent transactions with control premium, and calibrating to the prior round, applied to a fictional Series C case.

#### Silicon Valley Origin Story
- Silicon Valley grew from a 'fluke': Shockley (transistor Nobel winner) set up there to be near his sick mother.
    - Employees (the 'Traitorous Eight') left over his temper and seeded firms like Intel, AMD
- Alumni networks (e.g. the PayPal mafia) went on to found or fund Tesla, SpaceX, Uber, Meta.

#### Early vs Later Stage Valuation
- Later stage brings real numbers and listed peers; assumptions and comparables matter more than the formulas.
- Methods covered: trading multiples, precedent transactions, DCF, and scenario analysis (First Chicago method).
- Drawbacks: complex capital structures (liquidation preferences, share classes) and illiquidity discounts.
    - Square case preview: founders got about 0.5% at a roughly 3 billion exit
- Investors care more about IRR at later stages, when forecasts become meaningful.

#### Series C Case and Trading Multiples
- Fictional vertical software company (Organise): Series B June 2025, 30 million raised at 210 post-money.
    - Series C: 60 million at 450 pre-money for an 11.8% stake
    - NTM revenue 54 million, growth 50%, gross margin 76%, net cash 22 million
- EV = equity value + debt − cash; equity value = EV + cash − debt. With no debt, only cash matters.
- Rule of 40 (revenue growth + FCF margin) justifies negative FCF (−10) during heavy growth investment.
- Multiples from 8 peers: median 7.6x, regression on growth 10.4x (R² 74%), growth-adjusted 14.4x.
    - Pre-money equity = multiple × 54 × (1 − 0.20 DLOM) + 22 cash; DLOM range 15 to 25%

#### Precedent Transactions and Control
- Control premium study (US listed targets, 1980 to 2008): median 38%, average 46%; 35% assumed, range 25 to 45%.
- Precedent multiples are stripped of control premium; a buyer seeking control would add it back (×1.35).
- Clean valuation differs from willingness to pay, which adds for rights like liquidation preference, board seat, or top co-investors.
- Unicorn study of 135 firms: 56% average overvaluation of common shares; 65 worth below 1 billion after adjustment.

#### Calibrating to the Prior Round
- Steps: derive EV implied by the Series B, divide by revenue then, compare to peer multiple, get calibration factor.
- Apply factor (about 0.85) to the 10.4x regression multiple; no separate DLOM since it is embedded in the factor.
- Drawback: any bias in the previous round carries into the new valuation.
- Slide revenue figure was wrong; it should be the NTM revenue forecast as of June 2025.

### Domande — VC 28-10 — Stato del mercato VC (Q3 2026)
1. [Scelta multipla] Un'azienda si quota e il titolo sale del 40% nel primo giorno di trading. Cosa indica principalmente? — Opzioni: A) Che l'IPO è stata un grande successo per l'azienda, che incassa anche il rialzo | B) Underpricing da parte della banca: l'azienda incassa solo il prezzo primario d'offerta | C) Che il mercato era in bolla | D) Che gli LP del fondo VC hanno perso valore — Soluzione: B. L'azienda riceve solo il prezzo primario dell'IPO: un forte rialzo il primo giorno significa che le azioni sono state prezzate troppo basse (underpricing), lasciando 'soldi sul tavolo'.
2. [Scelta multipla] Quali affermazioni sul mercato VC nel Q3 2026 sono corrette secondo la lezione? — Opzioni: A) L'AI ha attratto circa il 64% dei finanziamenti del trimestre | B) Negli USA il 75% del capitale è andato al 5% dei deal (power law estrema) | C) I dati dei diversi provider coincidono perché esiste un obbligo di disclosure | D) L'88% del capitale raccolto va a gestori esperti, segnale di incertezza — Soluzione: A, B, D. I provider differiscono proprio perché nel private capital non c'è obbligo di disclosure. Nota: all'esame non verranno chiesti i numeri esatti di mercato, ma i concetti (concentrazione, power law, flight to experience).
3. [Scelta multipla] Per confrontare la dimensione del VC tra paesi/regioni, quale metrica è più appropriata? — Opzioni: A) Ammontare assoluto investito | B) Numero di round | C) VC investito / PIL | D) Numero di unicorni — Soluzione: C. Il rapporto VC/PIL normalizza per la dimensione dell'economia (es. Israele è ~10 volte più piccolo in valore assoluto ma molto rilevante in rapporto al PIL).
4. [Aperta] Spiega cosa significa che il VC segue una 'power law' e quali implicazioni ha per la strategia di portafoglio di un fondo (spray-and-pray vs scommesse concentrate nei megaround AI). — Traccia di soluzione: Pochi deal generano la gran parte dei rendimenti (una singola azienda può ripagare l'intero fondo). Implica: diversificare per avere esposizione ai pochi outlier (spray-and-pray funziona ancora per le app AI), ma i megaround AI richiedono ticket grandi e coinvolgimento hands-on, quindi scommesse più concentrate; inoltre il capitale si concentra (75% al 5% dei deal) e sui gestori esperti.

### Domande — VC 29-10 (parte 1) — Deal sourcing, screening, Berkus
1. [Scelta multipla] Secondo gli studi citati, qual è il principale canale di deal sourcing dei VC? — Opzioni: A) Algoritmi quantitativi su dati web | B) Network: altri VC, founder conosciuti, avvocati, eventi, enabler | C) Cold email dei founder | D) Piattaforme di crowdfunding — Soluzione: B. Il sourcing è soprattutto network-driven; nel paper 2020 solo circa il 2% dei deal era trovato in modo quantitativo (anche se la quota sta crescendo).
2. [Scelta multipla] Nel metodo Berkus riscalato visto in classe, qual è la valutazione massima pre-money? — Opzioni: A) 500K (100K per categoria) | B) 2,5 milioni (500K per ciascuna delle 5 categorie) | C) 3,5 milioni | D) Dipende dai comparables — Soluzione: B. Versione classica: 5 categorie × 100K = 500K max. Riscalata: 5 × 500K = 2,5M, perché il seed medio oggi è circa 3,5M.
3. [Scelta multipla] Quali sono le 5 categorie del metodo Berkus? — Opzioni: A) Sound idea, prototype, management team, strategic relationships, product rollout | B) Team, opportunity size, product, competition, sales | C) Management, stage, technology, litigation, legislation | D) TAM, SAM, SOM, CAC, LTV — Soluzione: A. La seconda opzione sono i fattori dello Scorecard, la terza alcuni fattori della Risk Factor Summation.
4. [Scelta multipla] Perché in Europa continentale le exit dei VC passano soprattutto da acquisizioni/CVC? — Opzioni: A) Perché le IPO sono vietate per le startup | B) Per mancanza di capitale late-stage e di percorsi di IPO | C) Perché i founder europei non vogliono quotarsi | D) Perché le CVC pagano sempre multipli più alti — Soluzione: B. Mancano capitale late-stage e mercati IPO per small cap; la capitalizzazione di borsa sul PIL è bassa (Italia ~47% vs UK ~200%).
5. [Aperta] Elenca i principali criteri di screening di un VC e spiega perché il team pesa circa la metà della decisione, soprattutto in early stage. — Traccia di soluzione: Criteri: fit con la strategia del fondo (stage, ticket, industria, geografia), team, prodotto, business model, mercato/TAM e opportunity size, key assumptions, competizione, adattabilità del management. Il team pesa ~50% perché in early stage mancano dati (ricavi, trazione): l'unica cosa valutabile è la capacità del 'jockey' di eseguire e adattarsi; la reputazione si verifica tramite referenze (es. founder che ha truffato un socio → deal scartato).

### Domande — VC 03-11 — Constellation Space, Risk Factor Summation, VC method

[Scelta multipla] Nella Risk Factor Summation con calibrazione classica, quanto vale ogni punto? — Opzioni: A) 100K su baseline 500K | B) 250K su baseline 2M | C) 500K su baseline 2,5M | D) 10% della baseline — Soluzione: B. ### Domande — VC 04-11 — Valutazione later-stage (multipli, precedent, calibrazione)
1. [Scelta multipla] Qual è la relazione corretta tra Enterprise Value ed Equity Value? — Opzioni: A) EV = equity value + debt − cash | B) EV = equity value − debt + cash | C) Equity value = EV + debt − cash | D) EV = equity value × (1 − DLOM) — Soluzione: A. Quindi equity value = EV + cash − debt. Nel caso Organise senza debito conta solo la cassa (+22M).
2. [Scelta multipla] Una SaaS cresce del 50% con FCF margin −10%. Rispetta la Rule of 40? — Opzioni: A) Sì: 50 + (−10) = 40 | B) No: il FCF è negativo | C) No: serve EBITDA positivo | D) Sì, ma solo se il gross margin supera il 76% — Soluzione: A. Rule of 40 = crescita ricavi % + FCF margin %. Giustifica FCF negativo in fasi di forte investimento in crescita.
3. [Scelta multipla] Se applichi multipli da precedent transactions per un investitore che NON acquisisce il controllo, cosa fai col control premium? — Opzioni: A) Lo aggiungi (×1,35) | B) Lo togli: i multipli di precedent transactions includono il premio di controllo | C) Lo ignori: non influisce | D) Lo sostituisci con il DLOM — Soluzione: B. Le transazioni precedenti incorporano un premio di controllo (mediana 38%, si assume 35%, range 25-45%): va depurato per una quota di minoranza; si riaggiunge solo se il buyer cerca il controllo.
4. [Scelta multipla] Nella calibrazione sul round precedente, perché NON si applica anche un DLOM separato? — Opzioni: A) Perché il DLOM è già incorporato nel fattore di calibrazione | B) Perché le aziende later-stage sono liquide | C) Perché il DLOM vale solo per le IPO | D) Perché il fattore di calibrazione è sempre maggiore di 1 — Soluzione: A. Il fattore (~0,85) confronta il multiplo implicito del round B con quello dei peer quotati, quindi cattura già sconto di illiquidità e altre differenze. Svantaggio: eventuali bias del round precedente si trascinano.
5. [Aperta] Calcola la pre-money equity value di Organise con il multiplo di regressione 10,4x, NTM revenue 54M, DLOM 20% e net cash 22M. — Traccia di soluzione: Pre-money equity = multiplo × revenue × (1 − DLOM) + cash = 10,4 × 54 × 0,8 + 22 = 449,28 + 22 ≈ 471M (vicino ai 450M pre-money proposti nella Series C).
